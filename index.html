AI 101: Introduction to AI Applications

3-Week Intensive Syllabus

How can we help beginners understand what artificial intelligence is and then gradually move toward building useful AI systems? This course provides an introduction to the main ideas behind modern AI, including machine learning, data science basics, natural language processing, large language models, Flowise, retrieval-augmented generation, and AI agents.

Prerequisites

  • Basic computer literacy
  • Light Python familiarity is helpful but not required
  • Curiosity about AI products and how they work

Highly recommended prerequisites: Not required, but it will be very useful to have a basic understanding of how machine learning works. Check out these resources to get acquainted with the basics:

  • 3Blue1Brown — Visual, intuition-first explanations of math and neural networks. Start with the neural networks series (Chapters 1–4).
  • Jurafsky & Martin, Speech and Language Processing (3rd ed.) — Free online textbook. We loosely follow parts of this book for the NLP and language modeling sections.
  • The Illustrated Transformer — A clear visual walkthrough of the Transformer architecture.
  • Neural Networks and Deep Learning (Chapters 1, 2, and 5) — A short free online book covering fundamentals of neural networks and backpropagation.

Learning objectives. Students will:

  1. Understand the difference between AI, machine learning, deep learning, NLP, RAG, and AI agents.
  2. Learn the key ideas behind data, features, models, tokenization, transformers, and LLMs.
  3. Understand how practical AI systems are built with retrieval, tools, and workflows.
  4. Design a simple website-ready AI project concept by the end of the 3 weeks.

Logistics

Format: Self-study or guided bootcamp

Length: 21 days (3 weeks)

Level: Beginner — no prior AI experience required

Track: AI fundamentals → NLP & LLMs → Flowise, RAG, and AI agents

Suggested tools: Python, Google Colab, pandas, scikit-learn, LLM API or local model, embeddings, vector database, Flowise

Expected outcome: Learners will understand core AI vocabulary and be able to outline a simple modern AI application end-to-end.

Readings

We will loosely follow this book for the NLP and language modeling portions of the course: Jurafsky & Martin (J&M), Speech and Language Processing, 3rd edition, available online for free. You may find it helpful to read the relevant chapters to prepare questions or to review content in more depth after each session.

Additional video resources and optional readings are linked in the schedule below. These are provided to supplement the course material for those interested in going deeper.

Core textbook

  • Jurafsky & Martin, Speech and Language Processing (3rd ed.) — Chapters on tokenization, n-grams, neural networks, transformers, and language models.

Video resources

  • 3Blue1Brown — Start with Neural Networks (Chapters 1–4) for visual intuition on how neural networks learn.
  • The Illustrated Transformer (Alammar, 2018) — A clear visual guide to the Transformer architecture.

Optional reading

  • Neural Networks and Deep Learning (Chapters 1, 2, 5) — Free online book on fundamentals.
  • The HuggingFace LLM Course — Practical introduction to working with large language models.

Course Schedule

Note: the order can be adjusted depending on your audience. This version is designed to gradually move from fundamentals to modern AI applications.

Day Topic Main Ideas Practice Readings
Week 1 — AI Foundations
1 What is AI? AI vs ML vs deep learning vs data science; real-world AI products; prediction vs generation List 5 AI products and explain what each one does. No readings.
2 Python for AI Beginners Variables, loops, functions, notebooks, Colab workflow Open a notebook and inspect a small dataset. No readings.
3 Data Science Basics Structured vs unstructured data; features; labels; train-validation-test split Choose target and input columns in a toy dataset. No readings.
4 Classification & Metrics Classification idea; accuracy; precision; recall; F1 Create a spam classification example and evaluate errors. J&M Ch. 4
5 How Models Learn Loss functions; gradient descent; overfitting vs generalization Explain why memorization is not real learning. 3B1B: Neural Nets
6 Neural Networks Layers; activations; embeddings; why deep learning matters Draw a tiny neural network for sentiment classification. J&M Ch. 5, Ch. 6
7 Week 1 Recap Connect AI concepts to a real use case Describe one real problem and an AI-based solution idea. Review week.
Week 2 — NLP, Transformers, and LLMs
8 What is NLP? Text classification, translation, summarization, question answering Write 10 NLP tasks with their inputs and outputs. J&M Ch. 2
9 Tokenization Words, characters, bytes, type-token distinction, BPE intuition Tokenize one sentence in three different ways. J&M Ch. 2
10 Language Modeling Next-token prediction, n-grams, probability of text Build a simple bigram table from a paragraph. J&M Ch. 3
11 RNNs & Sequence Models Sequential context, RNN intuition, LSTMs, long-context limitations Explain why earlier sequence models struggle with long text. J&M Ch. 13
12 Transformers & Attention Attention mechanism, parallelization, why transformers dominate Mark which words in a sentence should attend strongly to each other. J&M Ch. 8, Illustrated Transformer
13 Large Language Models Pretraining, autoregressive generation, GPT vs BERT vs T5 Compare one chatbot task and one classification task. J&M Ch. 7, Ch. 10
14 Prompting & LLM Use System prompts, zero-shot, few-shot, hallucinations, grounding Write a strong prompt for summarizing a report. J&M Ch. 9
Week 3 — Flowise, RAG, and AI Agents
15 From Models to Products Why raw LLMs are not enough; app architecture; external knowledge Draw a document Q&A application architecture. No readings.
16 What is RAG? Chunking, embeddings, vector stores, retrieval plus generation Break one document into chunks and think of likely questions. No readings.
17 RAG Design Choices Chunk size, overlap, top-k retrieval, grounded answers Compare poor chunking with good chunking. No readings.
18 Flowise Basics Chatflows, assistants, agentflows, visual AI orchestration Sketch a Flowise chatflow for a knowledge assistant. No readings.
19 Build a Flowise RAG Bot Documents, embeddings, vector store, LLM, testing grounded answers Outline a FAQ or internal document assistant. No readings.
20 AI Agents Tools, memory, actions, planning, when to use agents Design an agent that can search documents and answer questions. No readings.
21 Final Mini Project Choose a domain, describe the stack, explain the workflow Create a final project page with problem, solution, and architecture. No readings.

List of Topics

By the end of this course, you should be familiar with each of the following topics:

Foundations

  • Artificial intelligence vs machine learning vs deep learning
  • Prediction vs generation
  • Structured and unstructured data
  • Features and labels
  • Train-validation-test splits
  • Classification: accuracy, precision, recall, F1
  • Loss functions (cross-entropy, negative log likelihood)
  • Gradient descent and learning rates
  • Overfitting vs generalization
  • Neural networks: layers, activations, embeddings

Language AI

  • Natural language processing: tasks and applications
  • Tokenization: words, characters, bytes, BPE
  • The type–token distinction
  • N-gram language models
  • Recurrent neural networks and LSTMs
  • Attention and Transformers
  • Autoregressive language models (GPT)
  • Masked language models (BERT)
  • Sequence-to-sequence models (T5)
  • Pretraining and scaling laws
  • Prompting: zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought
  • Hallucinations and grounding

Applications

  • From models to products: app architecture
  • External knowledge and document retrieval
  • Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
  • Chunking strategies and chunk size
  • Embeddings and similarity search
  • Vector databases
  • Top-k retrieval and grounded answers
  • Flowise: chatflows, assistants, agentflows
  • Visual AI orchestration
  • AI agents: tools, memory, actions, planning
  • When to use agents vs simple retrieval
  • End-to-end project design

Tools and Platforms

  • Python and Google Colab
  • pandas and scikit-learn
  • LLM APIs and local models
  • Embedding models
  • Vector databases (Pinecone, Chroma, FAISS)
  • Flowise for visual pipeline building

Resources

Textbook

  • Jurafsky & Martin, Speech and Language Processing (3rd ed.) — Free online. Covers tokenization, language models, neural networks, transformers, and many NLP tasks in depth.

Video courses and visual guides

  • 3Blue1Brown — Beautiful visual explanations of math, linear algebra, and neural networks. The neural networks series and linear algebra series are excellent starting points.
  • The Illustrated Transformer (Alammar, 2018) — Step-by-step visual guide to understanding attention and Transformers.
  • The HuggingFace LLM Course — Hands-on introduction to working with large language models in Python.

Supplementary books and tutorials

  • Neural Networks and Deep Learning (Michael Nielsen) — Free online book. Chapters 1, 2, and 5 cover the fundamentals of backpropagation and training.
  • The official PyTorch tutorial — Learn the basics of tensor computation and building neural networks in PyTorch.
  • Stanford CS 230 PyTorch tutorial — A more compact PyTorch introduction.
